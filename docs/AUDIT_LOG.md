## Stage 7 - Stark (read-only investigation, no source change)
Findings: (1) implemented = E1 coupling inside the n=2 manifold only (8x8), E at any angle to B; absent = hyperfine, n>=3 polarizability, Stark-dependent QED. (2) Dipole is purely 2s<->2p (ss and pp blocks exactly 0); E||B conserves m_j ([Jz,z]=0), E perp B changes m_j by exactly 1. (3) Analytic checks: linear Stark -> +-3 a_mu eE with 4 zero levels, residual falls as 1/E (0.8 % at 5e4 V/cm, 0.2 % at 2e5); anticrossing gap = 1.0232 x 2 sqrt3 a_mu eE, the 2.3 % is exactly the 2P3/2 admixture at 573 G (coupling of the crossing pair computed from eigenvectors: 1.0232); neglected n>=3 quadratic term (Landau-Lifshitz parabolic formula) is 2e-6 MHz at 10 V/cm, 0.02 MHz at 1 kV/cm. (4) Category: all tests are implementation checks; no spectroscopic validation yet (needs published anticrossing positions/widths including hyperfine). (5) Decision: no code change justified; gap = hyperfine, validation target = a published hydrogen n=2 anticrossing measurement (not yet located).
Stage 7 status: Stark AUDITED (implementation + analytic-consistency checks only); spectroscopic validation OPEN. Lead supplied by the reviewer, NOT read by us: R. T. Robiscoe, Phys. Rev. 138, A22 (1965), n=2 level crossing observed near 605 G with a ~0.7 V/cm transverse field (observable = quenching of a metastable beam, not a resolved gap); earlier Shyn, Robiscoe, Williams used a single hyperfine state. Not a validation until observable, geometry, hyperfine preparation and transition probabilities are matched.

## Stage 8 - Diamagnetic term (read-only audit, no source change)
Findings (scratch script, not in repo): (1) src/diamagnetic.py is a stand-alone first-order estimate, NOT part of any Hamiltonian; it uses e^2B^2/(8 m_e) and a0, no reduced mass. With H_dia = e^2B^2 rho^2/(8 mu), rho in a_mu, hydrogen scales as (1+m_e/m_p)^3 = 1.00163 (+0.16 %); the exact two-body (pseudomomentum) reduction is still to be checked against the literature before it is called correct. (2) n=2 matrix elements: rho^2 = 2/3 r^2 (1 - P2(cos)), spin-free, parity-even, so no 2s-2p coupling; within n=2 it is exactly diagonal in |l m_l> (commutes with L_z, J_z): <rho^2> = 28 (2s), 12 (2p, m=0), 24 (2p, |m|=1) a0^2, checked by direct radial and angular integration. (3) n=2 shifts (MHz, m_e form): 0.0573 T: 2s 0.0014, 2p0 0.0006, 2p1 0.0012; 1 T: 0.42/0.18/0.36; 20 T: 167/71/143; 60 T: 1501/643/1286. Against mu_B B: 1.7e-6 at 0.057 T, 3e-4 at 1 T, 1.8e-3 at 60 T. Adding it to h_n2 moves the 573 G crossing by 2e-4 G (573.3707 -> 573.3709), negligible against the +-3 G test tolerance and against any Stark/hyperfine effect. Second-order (B^4) at 60 T ~ 5e-3 MHz. (4) Limitation for n>=3: rho^2 couples 3s-3d inside the shell; <3s|rho^2|3d0> = -42.43 a0^2 gives 253 MHz at 20 T and 2274 MHz at 60 T, comparable with the 3s-3d separation (~313 MHz, Lamb only), so the diagonal first-order numbers for n=3 are order-of-magnitude only; a degenerate-subspace treatment is needed there. The "0.02 cm^-1 at 20 T" statement is reproduced (0.025) but should be read in that light. (5) Independent references available now: 1s = B^2/4 hartree (already tested), <r^2> formula vs numerical radial integral (tested). Published strong-field hydrogen tables still to be located (candidates: Ruder, Wunner, Herold, Geyer, Atoms in Strong Magnetic Fields, 1994; Herold, Ruder, Wunner, J. Phys. B 14, 751 (1981) for the two-body reduction), unverified.
